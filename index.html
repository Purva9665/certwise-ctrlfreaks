How we check

1. Genuine?

Official verification linkReal certificates can be checked on the issuer's own site (Credly, coursera.org/verify, nptel.ac.in, Microsoft Learn...). We check the link is on that site.
Look-alike sitesA link that copies the issuer's name (like "coursera-verify.com") is flagged as fake.
Certificate IDsWe check the ID format (e.g. Red Hat 123-456-789) and send you to the official page to confirm it.
Fake universitiesThe issuer is checked against UGC's list of 32 fake universities (February 2026).
QR code, link or text from a file or cameraUpload a photo, screenshot or PDF (up to 5 MB), or point your phone camera at the QR code. If there is no QR code, we read the printed text for the name, link or ID. All of it happens in your browser.
Live check with the issuerFor Credly, Coursera and edX links we ask the issuer itself whether the certificate exists, and show what its record says. A made-up ID on a real site is caught. (Needs the CertWise server.)
No record at allWorkshop and participation certificates have no official record - anyone can make one, so we say so.

Questions people ask

Can CertWise prove that a certificate is genuine?

For Credly, Coursera and edX links we ask the issuer itself and show its answer (this needs the CertWise server). For other issuers we check that the link is on the issuer's official verification site, flag copy-cat sites and check UGC's list of fake universities. The last step is always the issuer's own page, where the holder's name is shown - so we never say "100% genuine".

Why does a genuine certificate get a low score?

Genuine is not the same as valuable. A real workshop certificate has no exam and no recognised issuer, so an employer has nothing to go on. The score looks at three things: who gives it, how it is earned, and whether its jobs are in demand.

Where do the prices, salaries and demand come from?

From the pages linked under every fact: official exam pages and published salary and hiring reports. A monthly refresh keeps a fact only if its exact words are on the source page.

Is my certificate uploaded anywhere?

No. The photo or PDF is read inside your browser and never leaves it. For a live check, only the certificate's link is sent to the issuer.

My certificate is not in the list. Can I still check it?

Yes. Paste its link: for Credly, Coursera and edX we read the certificate's name, its issuer and how it is earned straight from the issuer's record, and score it from that (this needs the CertWise server). For any other certificate, answer three quick questions - who issued it, how it was earned and whether it has a verification link - and it is scored the same way, clearly marked as based on your answers.

Does CertWise sell courses or take commission?

No. It is free for students, sells nothing and takes no commission, so it has no reason to push any certificate.
